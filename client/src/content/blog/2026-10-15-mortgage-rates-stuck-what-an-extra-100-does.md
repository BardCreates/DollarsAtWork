Mortgage rates didn't budge this week. That sounds like good news until you see where they're stuck: 7.4%, more than a full point higher than a year ago.

If you bought a home recently, or you're about to, that rate is baked into every payment for years. You can't change the rate today. But you can change how long you pay it. A small extra payment each month is one of the most reliable money moves there is.

### What happened this week

On October 15, Freddie Mac reported that the average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.40%. That's the same as last week and up from 6.27% a year ago. The 15-year fixed averaged 6.71%.

Rates have been pushed up by a rough stretch for inflation. Prices rose 3.7% over the past year in the latest government report, and the Federal Reserve raised its benchmark rate in September for the first time since 2023. Mortgage rates don't follow the Fed one-for-one. But they do tend to rise when investors expect higher rates for longer.

### What 1.13 points costs (example)

Let's take a made-up $350,000, 30-year loan. Here's principal and interest only, before taxes and insurance:

| Rate | Monthly payment | Total interest over 30 years |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 6.27% (a year ago) | $2,160 | about $427,000 |
| 7.40% (this week) | $2,423 | about $522,000 |

That's about $264 more a month and about $95,000 more over the life of the loan. Same house. Same loan amount. Different year.

You may be able to refinance if rates fall later. Nobody knows when that will be. In the meantime, there's a lever you control.

### The lever: extra principal

Every mortgage payment is split in two. Part pays interest. Part pays down the loan, called principal. Early in a loan, most of the payment is interest.

When you send extra money and mark it for principal, it goes straight at the balance. A smaller balance means less interest next month. And the month after. That effect stacks up over decades.

Here's what it does on our example 7.40% loan:

| Extra each month | Paid off | Time saved | Interest saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $0 | 30 years | — | — |
| $100 | 26 years, 3 months | 3 years, 9 months | about $78,800 |
| One extra payment a year | 23 years, 8 months | 6 years, 4 months | about $129,900 |
| $250 | 22 years, 4 months | 7 years, 8 months | about $156,200 |

*Example numbers, rounded. Your loan will differ.*

Read the $100 row again. About $1,200 a year cuts nearly four years of payments. That's the power of starting early in the loan, when the balance is biggest.

"One extra payment a year" is the same idea as paying half your mortgage every two weeks. You end up making 13 full payments a year instead of 12. Some people find it easier to send one-twelfth extra each month.

### Is paying it off early right for you?

An extra principal payment works like a guaranteed return equal to your mortgage rate. At 7.4%, that's a strong return with no market risk. But it isn't always the first job for your dollars. Before you prepay, check these:

- **Emergency fund first.** Money in your house is hard to get back out. Keep a cash cushion so a surprise bill doesn't land on a credit card.
- **Higher-rate debt first.** A card charging 20% or more is costing you far more than a 7.4% mortgage. Pay that off before you prepay the house.
- **Don't skip a workplace match.** If your employer matches retirement contributions, that match is usually worth more than prepaying.
- **Low-rate loans are a different story.** If you locked in at 3% years ago, the math for prepaying is much weaker. Your extra dollars may do more elsewhere.

### Five things to do this week

1. **Find your rate and balance.** They're on your latest mortgage statement or your servicer's site.
2. **Check for a prepayment penalty.** Most standard home loans don't have one, but your loan documents will say for sure.
3. **Learn how your servicer applies extra money.** Mark the extra as "principal only." Otherwise it may be held and applied to next month's payment, which saves you nothing.
4. **Pick a number you can keep.** $50, $100, $250. A small amount you send every month beats a big one you stop after three months.
5. **Give it a line in your budget.** If extra principal has its own category, it gets paid on purpose instead of from whatever is left over.

The same idea works for car loans and student loans too. Any fixed-rate loan with no prepayment penalty gets shorter and cheaper with extra principal.
